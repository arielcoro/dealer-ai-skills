# Dealer Sentiment Patterns

The recurring theme taxonomy and what each pattern typically indicates operationally. SKILL.md and REFERENCE.md reference this file. Use these patterns to tag themes and translate patterns into operational signals.

---

## Positive theme taxonomy

The recurring patterns that signal what dealers are doing well.

### Named-staff praise

A specific employee is named with positive sentiment. The most common positive pattern in dealer reviews. Star advisors, salespeople, and finance managers drive a measurable share of repeat business.

**Operational signal.** Recognize and retain the named staff. Multiple positive mentions of the same person across months indicates a high-leverage employee whose departure would be costly.

### Exceeded expectations

Customer expected a normal experience and got something better. Common phrasings: "above and beyond", "did not expect this level of service", "surprised by how well", "rare for a dealer".

**Operational signal.** Whatever produced the surprise is worth identifying. Often a specific process step (a thorough explanation, a complimentary loaner, a proactive follow-up) that the dealer can systematize.

### Fast resolution

Customer had an issue and the dealer solved it quickly. "They fixed it the same day", "called me back within an hour", "got me out the door fast".

**Operational signal.** Speed produces loyalty. If fast resolution is a recurring theme, the dealer's process discipline is paying off and should be protected.

### Transparent pricing

Customer felt the pricing was honest and clear. "No hidden fees", "the price they quoted was the price I paid", "they explained every line".

**Operational signal.** Transparent pricing is rare in auto retail. When customers consistently call this out, it is a brand differentiator worth amplifying in marketing.

### Easy paperwork

F&I or sales paperwork was smooth and quick. "In and out in 45 minutes", "they had everything ready", "no surprises in the contract".

**Operational signal.** Process discipline in F&I is showing up. Worth recognizing the F&I manager and the back-office team.

### Trade transparency

Trade-in offer felt fair and was explained. "They walked me through how they valued my trade", "the trade offer was actually fair", "did not lowball me".

**Operational signal.** Trade-in trust is one of the most contested signals in dealer reviews. Positive mentions indicate strong sales floor discipline.

### Service follow-through

Service department followed up after the visit. "Called me a week later to make sure everything was good", "checked in on my concern".

**Operational signal.** Follow-through is rare and high-value. Worth systematizing across the service team.

### Family-feel or personal relationship

Customer felt like they were treated as a person, not a transaction. "Treated me like family", "remembered me from last time", "knew my dog's name".

**Operational signal.** Often indicates a single-rooftop family-owned dealership or a long-tenured staff. Hard to replicate at scale. Worth protecting in expansion.

### Recommendation language

Customer explicitly recommends the dealer to others. "Would recommend to anyone", "telling all my friends", "this is where you should go".

**Operational signal.** Recommendation language is the strongest positive marker. Convert these customers into referral participants if a referral program exists.

## Negative theme taxonomy

The recurring patterns that signal what dealers are doing wrong.

### Long wait times

Service or sales wait was longer than expected. "Waited 2 hours for an oil change", "took all day for paperwork", "kept me waiting without explanation".

**Operational signal.** Either understaffing, overbooking, or process inefficiency. If clustered in a specific window (last 30 days), often a recent change in staffing or volume.

### Communication failure

Customer was not kept informed. "Nobody called me back", "vehicle was in for a week with no update", "had to keep calling to get any information".

**Operational signal.** Communication failures are the most common negative pattern in dealer reviews. Solvable with a daily-update SOP. The fix is process, not people.

### Pricing surprise

Price at signing was different than quoted. "Quoted me one number and the contract said another", "fees I did not know about", "the OTD was wrong".

**Operational signal.** F&I or sales floor discipline issue. Potentially a regulatory exposure (TILA, state consumer protection). The dealer should investigate immediately when this theme appears.

### Repeat repair

Customer had to return for the same issue. "Came back three times for the same problem", "they did not fix it the first time", "issue returned a week later".

**Operational signal.** Either a diagnostic accuracy problem (tech skill gap), a parts issue, or a communication issue (customer's actual problem misunderstood). Worth a technician-level review.

### Broken promise

Dealer promised something and did not deliver. "Said they would call and never did", "promised a loaner and there was none available", "told me the work was covered and then charged for it".

**Operational signal.** Trust damage. Worse than other complaints because the customer's specific expectation was set by dealer staff.

### Vehicle damaged in service

Customer says their vehicle came back damaged. "Scratch on the bumper that was not there before", "rim curbed", "interior dirty".

**Operational signal.** Service drive process gap. Often resolvable but requires immediate dealer response and resolution.

### Pressure or hard sell

Sales or F&I pressured the customer. "Tried to upsell me on warranties I did not want", "would not let me leave", "kept pushing add-ons".

**Operational signal.** F&I or sales floor compliance issue. Repeated mentions indicate a process or pay-plan problem.

### Dishonest accusation

Customer accuses the dealer of being dishonest. "They lied about", "bait and switch", "they hide the real price".

**Operational signal.** Serious. May be customer perception, may be operational reality. Either way it is a high-priority response and a possible regulatory exposure depending on specifics.

### Unreturned phone calls

Specific pattern of dealer not returning calls. "Left 4 messages, no callback", "BDC never returns my calls".

**Operational signal.** BDC or process gap. Often a CRM workflow issue (no automatic callback assignment, no follow-up gates) rather than individual laziness.

### Bait-and-switch advertising

Vehicle on website was different than what was available. "Vehicle was sold the day before but they still advertised it", "the price online was different from in-store".

**Operational signal.** Inventory feed accuracy or pricing-update process issue. Can also be regulatory exposure (state advertising laws).

### Negative named-staff mentions

A specific employee is named with negative sentiment. Less common than positive named mentions but more diagnostic when present.

**Operational signal.** Recurring negative mentions of the same person across reviews warrant a manager conversation. Single mentions may be customer-specific; recurring mentions are a pattern.

### Recall or warranty denial

Customer was told something was not covered when they expected it to be. "Said the warranty did not apply", "denied the recall fix".

**Operational signal.** Either OEM policy issue (legitimate denial) or service department escalation issue (mis-applied policy). Worth a service manager review.

### Wait-then-no-loaner

Customer was told they would get a loaner, but did not. "Said I could have a loaner but there were none", "promised a shuttle that never came".

**Operational signal.** Service drive logistics gap. Loaner fleet management or shuttle dispatch process.

### Aged inventory frustration

Customer arrived for a specific vehicle and it was not available. "Came in for the truck I saw online and they told me it was sold last week".

**Operational signal.** Inventory feed lag or sales floor coordination issue.

## Mixed theme indicators

When a review combines positive and negative elements, common patterns:

### "Great people, bad process"

Customer praises individual employees but criticizes a process. "Mike was great but the wait was too long" or "Carla did her best but the system kept failing".

**Operational signal.** Staff is performing despite a process issue. Fix the process to amplify the staff.

### "Good experience but would not return"

Customer reports a good single experience but states they would not come back. Usually a single specific issue that overshadows the otherwise positive interaction.

**Operational signal.** Investigate the specific issue. Loyalty signal matters more than satisfaction signal.

### "5-star experience with a 4-star caveat"

Customer rated 5 stars and mostly praises but adds a "but" sentence. Most dealers ignore the "but". The skill flags it because the "but" is the operational signal.

## Cross-pattern signals

Patterns that emerge when multiple themes co-occur.

### Communication failure + long wait

When both appear together, the operational signal is not "we are slow" but "we are slow and we are not telling customers". The fix is the daily-update SOP, not just speed.

### Pricing surprise + dishonest accusation

When both appear together, the legal exposure is real. The dealer's compliance officer should review the specific deals referenced.

### Named-staff praise (one person) + long wait

When the same staff member is praised but customers also complain about wait time, the issue is workload. The star performer is overloaded. Hire or redistribute.

### Department-wide negative + recent time window

When negatives cluster in one department in the last 30 to 60 days, the operational signal is "recent change". New hire, staffing departure, process change, parts supply issue.

## How to translate patterns to operational signals

The skill produces operational signals in the report by translating frequency and pattern combinations into recommendations. The translation rules:

**Single-mention themes** → note in the report, do not drive recommendations.
**3 to 5 mentions over the period** → flag as a watch signal.
**6+ mentions** → recommend operational action.
**Time-clustered mentions** → recommend investigating the recent change.
**Department-skewed mentions** → recommend department-level attention.
**Cross-department mentions** → recommend dealership-wide process review.

Operational signals should be specific. "Improve communication" is useless. "Establish a daily customer-update SOP for service drive customers with vehicles in for more than 24 hours" is actionable.

## What to flag for legal or compliance review

Some patterns may have regulatory implications. Surface them as legal-flag items separate from operational signals:

- Pricing surprise + specific deal references → potential TILA exposure
- Dishonest accusation with specific contract language → potential UDAP claim
- Discrimination allegations (any kind) → ADA, FHA, or state civil rights exposure
- Vehicle damage claims with cost references → potential bailment claim
- TCPA references (unwanted calls or texts) → potential TCPA exposure

The skill flags these as "compliance officer review recommended" without offering legal opinion.

## Use of the patterns in the report

The patterns in this file feed two parts of the report:

1. **Top themes section.** Match review content to patterns and rank by frequency.
2. **Operational signals section.** Translate pattern combinations and frequencies into specific actionable recommendations.

When a pattern in this file does not match the input, the skill notes it as a custom theme using the customer's own language. The taxonomy is comprehensive but not exhaustive. New dealer-specific patterns can emerge that warrant their own labels.
