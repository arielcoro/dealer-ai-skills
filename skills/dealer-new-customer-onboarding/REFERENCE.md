# Dealer New Customer Onboarding — Reference Framework

The program architecture, the sales-to-service handoff philosophy, and the why-90-days reasoning. SKILL.md references this file. Read it before designing any onboarding program.

---

## Why 90 days specifically

The 90-day window is not arbitrary. Several operational realities make it the right length.

**Day 0 to 30: The orientation window.** The customer is still adjusting to the vehicle, learning the connected services, and figuring out where to put the registration paperwork. Salesperson is still salient in memory. Brand experience is forming.

**Day 30 to 60: The first-service window.** Most OEMs schedule the first service at 5,000 to 7,500 miles, which most owners hit between day 45 and day 90. This is the moment that determines the long-term service relationship. If the customer comes back, they typically stay for years. If they don't, they typically never do.

**Day 60 to 90: The early-loyalty window.** The customer has formed an opinion about whether this dealership is "their" dealership. Reviews are written in this window. Referrals are made in this window. The customer's relationship trajectory is set.

By day 91, the relationship is established. Either the dealer is the customer's go-to or the customer has settled elsewhere. Changing the trajectory after day 90 is significantly harder and more expensive.

## The four phases of the 90-day program

The program is structured around four operational phases. Each phase has different goals, different touchpoint density, and different success metrics.

### Phase 1: Delivery (Day 0 to 1)

**Goal:** Set the relationship up correctly from minute one.

**Operational reality:** The customer is excited, distracted, signing papers, and trying to absorb a lot at once. Information density must be carefully controlled. Critical touchpoints include the OEM connected services activation (which is harder to get done later), the service advisor introduction (which most dealers skip), and a clear sense of "what happens next."

**Touchpoints:**
- In-person delivery walkaround with the OEM-specific connected services setup
- Service advisor introduction (named, with business card and direct phone)
- Welcome email within 24 hours
- First impressions survey

**Success metrics:**
- 100% OEM connected services activated at delivery
- 100% service advisor introduced and named
- Welcome email opened by 70%+ of customers

### Phase 2: Orientation (Day 2 to 30)

**Goal:** Reinforce the dealership relationship while the salesperson is still salient.

**Operational reality:** The customer is still in the orientation period. Touchpoints can be relationship-building rather than transactional. Service-side intro can happen in this window. Connected services usage check-in is high-leverage.

**Touchpoints:**
- Day 7 SMS check-in (BDC)
- Day 14 connected services usage check-in (email + optional in-person "service intro visit")
- Day 21 review request (if customer experience has been positive based on prior touchpoint engagement)

**Success metrics:**
- SMS reply or callback rate of 30%+
- Service intro visit booking (where geographically practical)
- Review request response rate of 25%+

### Phase 3: First service approach (Day 30 to 60)

**Goal:** Schedule and complete the critical first service appointment.

**Operational reality:** Most OEM-recommended first services fall in this window. This is the highest-leverage operational period in the entire program. Every touchpoint funnels toward "your first service is approaching."

**Touchpoints:**
- Day 30 first service reminder (email)
- Day 35 first service scheduling outreach (SMS if no booking yet)
- Day 45 second scheduling outreach (email + optional ringless voicemail)
- Day 55 personalized service appointment offer with available time slots

**Success metrics:**
- First service appointment scheduled within day 60 of delivery (target: 80%+)

### Phase 4: First service and beyond (Day 61 to 90)

**Goal:** Make the first service visit a relationship-building moment and set up the long-term cadence.

**Operational reality:** The customer is now physically in the dealership for service. This is the moment to build the long-term service relationship — not just complete the work.

**Touchpoints:**
- Day-of first service: in-person service experience including a personal hello from the service advisor (already introduced at delivery)
- Day-after first service: thank you + ask for review + introduce ongoing service cadence
- Day 80: 60-day post-service check-in (any issues, recommendations satisfaction)
- Day 90: 90-day milestone email (referral program introduction, anniversary cadence kickoff)

**Success metrics:**
- First service completed within day 90 (target: 70%+)
- Post-service review request response (target: 40%+)
- Customer satisfaction signal (whatever the dealer uses: NPS, CSI, direct survey)

## The sales-to-service handoff philosophy

This is the spine of the entire program. Done well, the dealer's service revenue from each customer is 5x to 10x the gross from the initial sale over the customer's ownership window. Done poorly, the dealer captures sale gross and nothing else.

### The five-step handoff

**Step 1: Service advisor introduction at delivery.** The salesperson walks the customer to the service drive at the end of delivery, introduces a specific service advisor by name, and the advisor hands the customer a business card with direct phone number. This takes five minutes and most dealers skip it.

**Step 2: Service intro visit (optional, within 14 days).** Customer is invited to "drop by" the service drive within two weeks for a no-work-required meet. The advisor shows them where to park for service, where the loaner office is, where the customer waiting area is. This is relationship investment. Practical for local customers; impractical for distant ones.

**Step 3: First service reminder cadence (day 30 onward).** Email and SMS reminders that the first service is approaching, with the named service advisor as the human contact.

**Step 4: First service appointment experience.** When the customer comes in, the service advisor greets them by name, references the delivery introduction, and treats the visit as relationship-building rather than just transactional. Loaner offered if needed. Wait area amenities pointed out.

**Step 5: Post-service follow-up.** Within 24 hours of service completion, a personal email or call from the service advisor thanking the customer and surfacing any concerns. Review request follows once concerns are addressed.

When all five steps happen, the customer's probability of becoming a long-term service customer is dramatically higher. Dealers that systematically execute steps 1, 3, and 4 typically capture 70%+ of new buyers as long-term service customers. Dealers that skip step 1 typically capture under 30%.

### Why most dealers skip step 1

Cultural, not operational. The salesperson considers the customer "delivered" once paperwork is signed and keys are handed over. Walking the customer to service feels like extra work the salesperson is not paid for (often literally, since sales commission structures rarely reward post-delivery work).

Fixing this requires:
- Sales pay plan adjustment (small but real incentive for the day-90 retention outcome)
- Sales process SOP that builds the handoff into delivery as a non-optional step
- Service advisor capacity at delivery moments (the service team has to be staffed for these handoffs)
- GM or dealer principal enforcement until the cultural norm shifts

The skill produces the program design. The cultural shift is the dealer's responsibility.

## OEM connected services activation

A critical operational detail many dealers handle poorly. Each OEM has a specific activation flow. The 90-day program treats activation as a first-class touchpoint.

### Why activation matters

- **Customer engagement.** Activated customers use the brand's app, which reinforces brand and dealer connection.
- **Data signal.** The OEM gets vehicle usage data, which feeds into the OEM's relationship marketing. Some of that relationship marketing routes back through the dealer.
- **OEM compliance.** Some OEM dealer agreements include activation rate targets.
- **Future service reminders.** The connected services app handles many OEM-driven service reminders. If activated, the customer gets recurring touchpoints from the OEM. If not, the dealer is the customer's only reminder source.

### Activation channels in the program

- **Delivery (day 0):** In-person guided activation. Highest success rate.
- **Day 14:** Email reminder + tutorial video if activation incomplete at delivery.
- **Day 30:** SMS prompt if connected services usage signal is low.

Activation rate target: 90%+ within 30 days.

## How the program scales by dealership type

The 90-day program is operationally demanding. Different dealer types adopt different versions.

### Single rooftop, high-touch family-owned

Full program executes. In-person service intro visit is highly practical. Personalized touchpoints from named staff (salesperson, dealer principal, service manager) are high-leverage.

### Multi-rooftop group with centralized BDC

BDC handles touchpoints centrally. In-person service intro visits handled per-rooftop. Personalization happens through CRM merge fields and rooftop-specific staff names.

### High-volume single rooftop

BDC capacity is the constraint. The program may compress touchpoints to the highest-leverage ones (delivery handoff, day 30 reminder, day 60 reminder, day 90 follow-up). Some touchpoints (day 7 SMS, day 14 service intro) may not be feasible at volume.

### Smaller used-vehicle dealer

Connected services activation is N/A. First service reminder cadence is still relevant (used vehicles need service too). Sales-to-service handoff still applies.

The skill recommends a version of the program based on the dealer's situation; the user can adjust further.

## Why this program pairs with email flows (not duplicates)

`dealer-email-flows` covers the broader lifecycle email program (year 1 through year 5+). This skill covers the intensive first 90 days specifically and includes non-email touchpoints (in-person, SMS, ringless voicemail).

The handoff between the two skills:
- Day 91: New customer onboarding program ends.
- Day 91 onward: Lifecycle email program continues.

There is no overlap. The onboarding skill is a focused 90-day program; the email flows skill is the broader ongoing relationship.

When both are deployed together, the customer has a continuous touchpoint cadence from day 0 to year 5+. When only onboarding is deployed, the customer goes dark after day 90 and may need to be re-engaged later. When only email flows is deployed, the critical first 90 days are weak and the long-term lifecycle has to compensate.

Both work better together. Build both.

## What this skill does not do

- It does not execute the program. The dealer's CRM, SMS platform, and BDC run the touchpoints.
- It does not measure customer satisfaction or NPS directly. The dealer's existing tools do that.
- It does not handle service capacity planning. The dealer's service operations team does that.
- It does not replace the sales pay plan changes that drive cultural buy-in.
